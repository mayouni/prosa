Live court

The live court of Prosa

The live court of Prosa is the reference court running in this page: it judges the declaration and the text below and prints its verdict at every change. It lets a reader change a rule or a sentence and see which code the court raises.

Two editors and the verdict of the court

The left editor holds a .proz declaration, and the right one carries the Markdown text of its piece. The court judges both in this browser: the page sends nothing to a server.

Press Escape, then Tab, to leave an editor with the keyboard.
The court reads this text as the file the piece names.

The three examples and the verdict each one gives

    
    
      
  • The example of a term declares the word "programmer" and refuses its synonym: the court raises one error, TERM_SYNONYM, on line 3 of the text.
    • 
      
  • The example of the affirmative rule declares a public page under the reader's rights: the court raises one error, LACK_LISTED, on the line that lists a lack.
    • 
      
  • The example of a profile declares two bounds on the voice: the court raises two warnings, OFF_PROFILE, one for the long sentences and one for the colons the profile asks for.
    • 
    

    
Each example becomes lawful when its text is repaired: the court then prints a count of zero errors and zero warnings.

  






  

    
How the page runs the reference court

    
The page loads prosa.js, the same file the command prosa runs under Node, and the contract of diagnostics. It parses the declaration, checks it at level 2, and judges the text against the first PIECE of the declaration.

    
A line USE "prosa:reader_rights" reads the constitution, which the page carries with it. A declaration that spans several files is pasted into the left editor as one text.
